# Design direction

## Grounded directions

The audience moves between the vertical TikTok scene and a live operator's desk. The product's cultural home is Vietnamese Buddhist temple practice, with its stage, prayer, and merit records. These directions cover lacquer, wood, textile, ceramic, bronze, and paper.

1. **Sơn son thếp vàng altar panels.** The temple stage and carved panels identify the setting at a glance. Use deep lacquer, old gold, and symmetrical panels around a clear live scene. The first view keeps the Buddha and altar in the center, with event state and participant merit at the edges. Its risk is repeating the current gold-heavy treatment.
2. **Sổ công đức.** The merit register connects real contributions to a visible total. Use a calm ruled ledger, ink names, and restrained red marks for new entries. The first view makes live status and the current event readable above a short contribution list. Its risk is making the live ritual feel like a spreadsheet.
3. **Hát bội stage curtain and program.** The painted theatre stage links a live performance with named cues. Use painted textile, dark borders, and a cue list that gives the central scene room. The first view shows the active ritual as a stage cue without covering the performers. Its risk is borrowing a theatre tradition that is not the product's own.
4. **Mộc bản kinh.** Temple woodblocks make repeated prayer text feel deliberate and legible. Use charcoal ink, carved spacing, and narrow vertical columns. The first view lets the live event lead while short readable captions carry the prayer and merit. Its risk is letting historical typesetting reduce modern control legibility.
5. **Bát Tràng incense vessels.** The ceramic tradition ties ritual objects to repeatable forms. Use pale celadon, mineral glaze, and a few strong vessel silhouettes. The first view assigns one clear place to each live ritual. Its risk is making controls look like an object catalog.
6. **Đại hồng chung.** The bronze temple bell gives the live scene a strong audio and event rhythm. Use dark bronze, a single strike mark, and ample quiet around event names. The first view connects a new live action to its sound and merit change. Its risk is under-serving controls that do not ring the bell.
7. **Sớ cầu an.** The Vietnamese prayer petition relates a viewer's wish to the action that carries it through the ritual. Use warm, uncoated paper, charcoal ink, a restrained cinnabar seal, and modern Vietnamese text. The first view keeps the live temple scene dominant and places the active wish and merit record on a legible petition strip. Its risk is looking like a static document unless live changes remain visible.

## Assigned direction

The direction roll assigned candidate 7. The chosen world is a working Vietnamese prayer petition, not a poster of one. The stage remains the main experience. Paper-colored plaques and strips carry live status, the active prayer, and merit. Cinnabar marks the current or connected state. Charcoal carries all reading text. Antique gold stays in the existing scene art and appears sparingly in interface accents. The admin controls use the same paper, ink, and seal language, arranged for operation rather than dressed as a scroll.

### First viewport

The 9:16 temple scene remains the visual center. The top strip names the temple and shows the TikTok connection state. The lower petition strip shows the active viewer and action, followed by the three existing viewer actions and the merit list. In the operator view, connection and audio settings lead, ritual controls follow, and simulation plus the RPC response stay easy to find.

### Visitor path

Viewers see the temple first, then the current participant and their action. Operators connect the room, unlock and tune sound, then trigger or simulate a ritual. The full control panel remains one link away from the quick controls.

### Signature interaction

A live prayer appears in the active petition strip while the existing scene animation carries the ritual. Its contribution appears in the merit list. The interface adds no new ritual or backend state.

### Cross-surface reach

Use the petition's paper and ink as the shared material. The live scene uses concise overlays; the quick dock and admin page use clear functional sections, direct action labels, and ledger-like event rows.

### Honest risk

Paper can lose contrast against the bright temple image. Keep live text on opaque warm-paper surfaces and keep the scene unobscured.

## Challenger review

The six catalog challengers were compared only on audience identification and product clarity.

| Challenger | Audience identification | Product clarity | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Versailles garden | Lower. Its French court setting is distant from Vietnamese temple practice. | Lower. Garden routes do not explain live rituals. | Declined |
| Brick build instructions | Lower. The toy-building world does not fit the audience. | Equal. Ordered instructions could explain setup and actions. | Competitive |
| Cracktro title screen | Lower. Its retro-computing identity does not fit the temple audience. | Lower. Depth-based motion obscures the event record. | Declined |
| Teletext service | Lower. The broadcast structure fits live media, but the cultural link is weak. | Higher. A compact fixed index can separate live state and event types. | Competitive |
| Doujin event catalog | Lower. Its convention culture is unrelated to the product. | Lower. A dense catalog would compete with the live scene. | Declined |
| Airline timetable | Lower. Its travel context does not fit temple ritual. | Equal. Named states and aligned counts would aid the operator. | Competitive |

Declined systems still contribute one structural discipline. From Versailles, use one clear reading axis from live status to participant and merit. From the cracktro, keep the foreground reading surface still while the ritual animation stays in the scene. From the event catalog, keep each event row's user, action, and merit together. These are structural rules, not borrowed decoration.
